Playbook

How a round runs

Result-driven, not time-boxed. A round ends when the data says so — via Bayesian winner call, spend ceiling, or fatigue — and a new angle takes the loser’s slot.

The loop

Every round runs the same seven-step cycle. Scan → propose 4 → pick 3 → ship → monitor gates → call winner/loser → replace. The competitor set is an explicit input; it evolves round-over-round.

  [1] Competitor scan  →  [2] Propose 4 angles  →  [3] Pick 3 live + 1 bench
                                                                   ↓
  [7] Retire loser, promote bench  ←  [6] Gate 2: call winner  ←  [4] Ship
           ↑                                 ↑                                ↓
           ← [1] Re-scan competitors  ←  [5] Gate 1: early cull checks

The two-gate architecture

Each round has two decision gates. Gate 1 is an early cull that keeps dud ads from eating budget. Gate 2 is the winner call that ends the round. Both are triggered by data, not by the calendar.

Gate 1 — Early cull

Trigger: any approach’s CPL crosses €3 after its first €500 of spend.

Action: cull that ad. We don’t wait for CPA signal if the lead cost is already out of range — downstream conversion won’t rescue a bad top-of-funnel number.

Guardrail: don’t cull before €500 spent on that approach. Below that, the CPL estimate is too noisy.

Exception: if all three approaches trip Gate 1, the issue is the audience, not the creative — pause the round and re-scope before respending.

Gate 2 — Winner call

Trigger (any one ends the round):

  • Any approach reaches Bayesian prob-to-be-best ≥ 90% on CPA — that’s the winner.
  • Any approach reaches €5k cumulative spend without a clear winner — call it on current ranking; the ceiling is a hard stop.
  • Any approach’s frequency exceeds 3 — fatigue is now confounding the signal; call it on what you have.

Action: mark winner & loser in the tracker. The middle stays live into the next round unchanged. The loser is retired.

Budget & audience

Budget split

Equal split across the three live approaches. We’re testing positioning, not optimising inside a proven angle — unequal weighting muddies the signal.

Recommended per-approach budget: €1.5k – €5k per round. Under €1.5k you won’t reach Gate 2 in a reasonable window; over €5k you’re violating the spend ceiling.

Placement: Advantage+ across Facebook and Instagram feed, Stories, Reels. 4:5 and 1:1 run feed; 9:16 runs Stories/Reels. No Audience Network.

Audience

One broad prospecting audience per round. Don’t stack audiences — you want the positioning signal clean. Audience testing is a separate track once a winner is locked.

Current round: broad trial acquisition (producer-leaning interest stacks + lookalikes of trial starters).

Spend parity: within 10% across the three approaches. If one spent far less, the pacing was off — re-run that arm before calling the round.

Metrics

Metric Used for Threshold
CPA on trial start Gate 2 winner call (primary) Bayesian prob-to-be-best ≥ 90%
CPL Gate 1 early cull > €3 after €500 spent ⇒ cull
Cumulative spend Gate 2 ceiling stop €5k per approach ⇒ force call
Frequency Gate 2 fatigue stop > 3 ⇒ force call
Trial-to-paid conversion rate Secondary — escalate if winner’s hold rate is weak —
CTR, CPM Diagnostic — understand why an approach won or lost Don’t call on these alone

Current NI 360 baseline CPA: €80. Win metric is lowest CPA with statistically meaningful volume; an approach needs ≥ 40 trial starts in the round to be eligible for winner status.

Retiring the loser

  1. Mark the loser’s Status = Retired on the tracker’s Ad Tests tab.
  2. Log the round result on the Decision Log tab: dates, each approach’s CPA / CPL / trial volume / frequency, winner, loser, one-line hypothesis for why.
  3. Don’t delete the retired brief or template — keep them for reference. If a competitor starts running that angle six months later, we may revive it.
Keep three approaches live at all times. Never retire two in the same round — you lose the baseline. If two approaches tie on CPA, both stay; retire only the clear loser.

The replacement approach

Rules for the new angle that replaces the loser:

  1. It must attack a gap the current round exposed. If all three losing approaches leaned on X and the competition is also leaning on X, the new angle avoids X.
  2. It must be meaningfully distinct from the two survivors. Don’t replace a price-anchor loser with a slightly-better price anchor — that’s an iteration, not a new angle. Save iterations for within-approach variants once a winner is locked.
  3. Source it from the fresh competitor scan. Abandoned positions are gifts — if a competitor has dropped a stance, claim it fast.

The replacement comes from the bench angle (the 4th proposed in the previous round that didn’t go live). If the bench is a good fit for the new gap, promote it; otherwise, brief a fresh 4th from the scan and put the bench back on standby.

When to stop rotating (locked winner)

After three consecutive rounds where the same approach wins, stop rotating. At that point the framework’s positioning-test job is done for that audience. Shift to:

  • Within-approach creative variants (headline, hero image, CTA swaps)
  • Audience expansion / lookalike testing using the locked approach as the control
  • Placement-specific optimisation (9:16 native creative vs resized 4:5)

Run the framework again when you open a new audience (e.g. mixing engineers for iZotope MPS Pro, different territory for PA Subscription).

Running the competitor scan

Do this at the start of every round — not just when a loser is retired.

  1. Pull the Meta Ad Library for each competitor in the scan set (see Competitor Queue). Filter to ads active in the last 14 days.
  2. Capture 3–5 top-running creatives per competitor — the ones running for weeks signal spend commitment.
  3. For each, log: format, visual pattern, headline, CTA, price display, UI-vs-lifestyle imagery.
  4. Roll findings into the “Territory claimed” section.
  5. The “Territory NOT claimed” section is what feeds the next approach brief.

Time budget: 45–60 minutes. Scanning endlessly is procrastination — the framework is about moving.
