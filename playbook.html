Playbook

How a round runs

Result-driven, not time-boxed. A round ends when the data says so — via Bayesian graduate calls, spend ceiling, or fatigue — and any approach that clears the bar graduates into the CPA campaign. Losers are retired, new angles take the open slots.

The loop

Every round runs the same seven-step cycle. Scan → propose 4 → pick 3 → ship → monitor gates → graduate winners / retire losers → replace. The competitor set is an explicit input; it evolves round-over-round.

  [1] Competitor scan  →  [2] Propose 4 angles  →  [3] Pick 3 live + 1 bench

The two-gate architecture

Each round has two decision gates. Gate 1 is an early cull that keeps dud ads from eating budget. Gate 2 is the graduate call that moves winners into the CPA campaign — one approach, two, or all three if they clear the bar. The CPA campaign wants creative volume, so multiple winners per round are a feature, not a problem. Both gates are triggered by data, not by the calendar.

Gate 1 — Early cull

Trigger: any approach’s CPL crosses €3 after its first €500 of spend.

Action: cull that ad. We don’t wait for CPA signal if the lead cost is already out of range — downstream conversion won’t rescue a bad top-of-funnel number.

Guardrail: don’t cull before €500 spent on that approach. Below that, the CPL estimate is too noisy.

Exception: if all three approaches trip Gate 1, the issue is the audience, not the creative — pause the round and re-scope before respending.

Gate 2 — Graduate call

Graduation bar (any one on its own qualifies an approach):

  • Reaches Bayesian prob-to-be-best ≥ 90% on CPA vs baseline — clear winner.
  • Posts CPA at or below baseline (€80) with ≥ 40 trial starts — significant volume at target, graduate it.
  • Reaches €5k cumulative spend while holding CPA ≤ baseline — spend ceiling hit, but the number held — graduate on what we have.

Round ends when every live approach has either graduated, been culled, or hit the spend ceiling without qualifying. Frequency > 3 forces an end call on any live approach — fatigue is now confounding signal.

Action: Leo moves all graduates into the CPA campaign (no one-winner filter — 1, 2, or 3 can graduate). Clear losers are retired. Bench angle promotes; fresh briefs fill the remaining open slots.

Why multi-winner matters. The CPA campaign is hungry for creative variety — ad fatigue is its biggest failure mode. If a round gives us two or three approaches at or below baseline, we don’t pick a favourite; we ship them all. The framework’s job is to harvest high-performing creative for the always-on campaign, not to declare a single monthly victor.

Budget & audience

Budget split

Equal split across the three live approaches. We’re testing positioning, not optimising inside a proven angle — unequal weighting muddies the signal.

Recommended per-approach budget: €1.5k – €5k per round. Under €1.5k you won’t reach Gate 2 in a reasonable window; over €5k you’re violating the spend ceiling.

Placement: Advantage+ across Facebook and Instagram feed, Stories, Reels. 4:5 and 1:1 run feed; 9:16 runs Stories/Reels. No Audience Network.

Audience

One broad prospecting audience per round. Don’t stack audiences — you want the positioning signal clean. Audience testing is a separate track once a winner is locked.

Current round: broad trial acquisition (producer-leaning interest stacks + lookalikes of trial starters).

Spend parity: within 10% across the three approaches. If one spent far less, the pacing was off — re-run that arm before calling the round.

Metrics

Metric Used for Threshold
CPA on trial start Gate 2 graduate call (primary) Bayesian prob-to-be-best ≥ 90%, OR CPA ≤ baseline with ≥ 40 trial starts
CPL Gate 1 early cull > €3 after €500 spent ⇒ cull
Cumulative spend Gate 2 ceiling stop €5k per approach ⇒ graduate if CPA ≤ baseline, else retire
Frequency Gate 2 fatigue stop > 3 ⇒ force end call on that approach
Trial-to-paid conversion rate Secondary — escalate if a graduate’s hold rate is weak —
CTR, CPM Diagnostic — understand why an approach won or lost Don’t call on these alone

Current NI 360 baseline CPA: €80. Graduation metric is CPA at or below baseline with statistically meaningful volume; an approach needs ≥ 40 trial starts in the round to be eligible to graduate.

Graduating winners & retiring losers

At Gate 2, every live approach gets one of three fates:

  1. Graduate — CPA ≤ baseline with volume, or Bayesian prob-to-be-best ≥ 90%. Mark Status = Graduated on the tracker’s Ad Tests tab. Leo moves the asset into the CPA campaign within 48h.
  2. Retire — CPA materially above baseline, or spend ceiling hit without qualifying. Mark Status = Retired. Keep the brief and template in the repo for reference.
  3. Middle — not yet at graduation bar but not clearly losing. Stays live into the next round unchanged, counted as a carry-over.

Log every round’s outcome on the Decision Log tab: dates, each approach’s CPA / CPL / trial volume / frequency, graduate(s), retired, one-line hypothesis for why each.

Multiple graduates per round is a feature. The CPA campaign needs creative variety to outrun fatigue. If two approaches hit the bar, both ship. Don’t pick a favourite.
Keep momentum in the test. If a round graduates all three approaches, great — Round N+1 tests three fresh angles against the fresh competitor scan. If it graduates zero and retires three, the audience or the baseline is the issue — escalate to Jordan before respending.

The replacement approaches

Rules for the new angles that fill open slots (graduated or retired):

  1. Each new angle must attack a gap the current round exposed. If the retired approaches leaned on X and the competition is also leaning on X, the new angles avoid X.
  2. Each must be meaningfully distinct from any carry-overs and from each other. Don’t replace a price-anchor retiree with a slightly-better price anchor — that’s an iteration, not a new angle. Save iterations for within-approach variants once a graduate is in CPA.
  3. Source them from the fresh competitor scan. Abandoned positions are gifts — if a competitor has dropped a stance, claim it fast.

The first replacement is the bench angle from the previous round (the 4th proposed that didn’t go live). Any remaining open slots get briefed fresh from the scan. Refresh the bench for the next round.

When to stop rotating (locked angle in CPA)

Once an approach has graduated into the CPA campaign, it’s treated as “locked” for the positioning test — we don’t re-run the same angle against itself. Ryan tracks its ongoing CPA in Post-Grad Watch. The framework’s positioning-test job is done for that angle on that audience — shift to:

  • Within-approach creative variants (headline, hero image, CTA swaps) to refresh the graduate in CPA before it fatigues.
  • Audience expansion / lookalike testing using the graduated angle as the control.
  • Placement-specific optimisation (9:16 native creative vs resized 4:5).

Run the framework again when you open a new audience (e.g. mixing engineers for iZotope MPS Pro, different territory for PA Subscription), or when a graduated angle’s CPA drifts > 15% from its winning round.

Running the competitor scan

Do this at the start of every round — not just when a loser is retired.

  1. Pull the Meta Ad Library for each competitor in the scan set (see Competitor Queue). Filter to ads active in the last 14 days.
  2. Capture 3–5 top-running creatives per competitor — the ones running for weeks signal spend commitment.
  3. For each, log: format, visual pattern, headline, CTA, price display, UI-vs-lifestyle imagery.
  4. Roll findings into the “Territory claimed” section.
  5. The “Territory NOT claimed” section is what feeds the next approach brief.

Time budget: 45–60 minutes. Scanning endlessly is procrastination — the framework is about moving.
